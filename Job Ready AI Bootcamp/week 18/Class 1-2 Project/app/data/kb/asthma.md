# Asthma

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## What asthma is
Asthma is a long-term condition in which the airways become swollen and narrow, causing wheezing, coughing, chest tightness and shortness of breath. Symptoms often come and go and can be worse at night or with exercise. With good care most people with asthma live normal, active lives.

## Common asthma triggers
Common triggers include colds and flu, dust and dust mites, smoke from tobacco or cooking fires, air pollution, pollen, pets, cold air, strong smells and exercise. Knowing your triggers and avoiding them where possible helps keep asthma under control.

## Reliever and preventer inhalers
A reliever inhaler works quickly to open the airways during symptoms. A preventer inhaler is used every day, even when you feel well, to reduce swelling in the airways over time. Using your reliever more often than usual is a sign that your asthma is not well controlled and you should book a review.

## Using an inhaler and spacer
Good technique gets the medicine into the lungs. A spacer makes most inhalers easier to use and is recommended for children. Ask a nurse or pharmacist to check your technique at each review, and rinse your mouth after using a preventer inhaler.

## Asthma action plan
Everyone with asthma should have a written asthma action plan from their clinician that says what to do every day, when symptoms get worse, and in an emergency. An asthma attack where the reliever does not help, the person cannot speak in full sentences, or the lips turn blue is an emergency.
