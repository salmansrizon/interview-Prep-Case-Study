# Diarrhoea

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## Common causes of diarrhoea
Sudden diarrhoea is usually caused by a viral infection or by contaminated food or water. It normally settles within a few days. The main risk is dehydration, not the diarrhoea itself.

## Fluids and food during diarrhoea
Drink more fluids than usual, and use oral rehydration solution (ORS) to replace lost salts. Keep eating small, regular meals of normal food. Children should continue breastfeeding or their usual milk. A clinician may advise zinc supplements for young children with diarrhoea.

## Stopping diarrhoea from spreading
Wash hands with soap after using the toilet, after changing nappies and before preparing food. Drink safe water, eat food that is freshly cooked and hot, and keep toilets clean. Do not prepare food for others while you have diarrhoea.

## When to seek care for diarrhoea
Contact the clinic if diarrhoea lasts more than 3 days in an adult or more than 1 day in a young child, or if there is a high fever or severe stomach pain. Seek urgent care for blood in the stool, signs of dehydration, or a baby who is not feeding.
