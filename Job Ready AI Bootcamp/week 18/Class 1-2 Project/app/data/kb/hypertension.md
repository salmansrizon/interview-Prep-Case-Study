# High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## What blood pressure numbers mean
Blood pressure is written as two numbers, for example 120/80 mmHg. The top number is the pressure when the heart beats, and the bottom number is the pressure between beats. High blood pressure usually has no symptoms, which is why regular checks matter. Your clinician decides what your target should be.

## Why high blood pressure matters
Over many years, high blood pressure damages blood vessels. It raises the risk of stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney disease and eye problems. Keeping blood pressure under control lowers these risks.

## Lifestyle steps for healthy blood pressure
Eat less salt and fewer salty snacks, eat more vegetables and fruit, stay active most days, keep a healthy weight, limit alcohol and stop smoking. Good sleep and managing stress also help. These steps support any medicine your clinician prescribes.

## Checking blood pressure at home
If your clinician asks you to check at home, sit quietly for five minutes first, keep your arm supported at heart level, and do not talk during the reading. Use a validated upper-arm monitor and write down the results with the date and time to bring to your appointment.

## Blood pressure medicines
Take blood pressure medicines exactly as prescribed, even when you feel well. Do not stop or change them on your own. If you think a medicine is causing side effects, or you have missed doses, speak to your clinician or pharmacist.
