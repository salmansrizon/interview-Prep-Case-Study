# Heat Illness

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## Heat exhaustion
Heat exhaustion can happen during very hot weather or hard work in the heat. Signs include heavy sweating, tiredness, headache, dizziness, nausea, muscle cramps and a fast pulse. Children, older adults and outdoor workers are at higher risk.

## Cooling down
Move to a cool or shaded place, loosen clothing, lie down and raise the legs slightly, sip water or oral rehydration solution, and cool the skin with water and a fan. Symptoms usually improve within 30 minutes.

## Heatstroke is an emergency
Heatstroke is life-threatening. Call 999 if someone is still unwell after 30 minutes of cooling, or has a very high body temperature, hot skin that has stopped sweating, confusion, a seizure or loss of consciousness. Keep cooling them while you wait for help.

## Preventing heat illness
During hot weather, drink water regularly, avoid hard work or exercise in the hottest part of the day, wear light, loose clothing, rest in the shade and check on older neighbours and young children. Never leave a child or pet in a parked vehicle.
