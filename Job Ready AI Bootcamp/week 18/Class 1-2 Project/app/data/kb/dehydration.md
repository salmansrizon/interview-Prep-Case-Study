# Dehydration and Oral Rehydration

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## Signs of dehydration
Dehydration means the body has lost more water and salts than it has taken in, often from diarrhoea, vomiting, fever or heat. Early signs are thirst, a dry mouth, dark yellow urine, passing urine less often, tiredness and dizziness when standing up. Babies may have fewer wet nappies and cry without tears.

## Oral rehydration solution (ORS)
Oral rehydration solution, or ORS, replaces both water and salts and is the first treatment for mild to moderate dehydration, especially from diarrhoea. Give small, frequent sips rather than large amounts at once. Keep breastfeeding babies and keep offering normal food once the person can eat.

## Preparing ORS safely
Mix an ORS packet with exactly the amount of clean, safe drinking water written on the packet. Too little water makes the solution too salty. Do not add sugar, juice or milk to ORS. Use the mixed solution within the time stated on the packet, usually 24 hours, and throw away what is left.

## Danger signs of dehydration
Get urgent medical care if a person is very drowsy or confused, cannot keep any fluids down, has not passed urine for 8 hours or more, has sunken eyes, or has blood in the stool. Babies and older adults become dangerously dehydrated faster, so seek care early for them.
