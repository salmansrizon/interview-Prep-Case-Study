# Type 2 Diabetes

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## What type 2 diabetes is
In type 2 diabetes the body does not use insulin well, so the level of sugar (glucose) in the blood stays too high. It develops slowly and is more likely with older age, a family history, being overweight and low physical activity. It is diagnosed with blood tests ordered by a clinician.

## Common symptoms of diabetes
Many people have no symptoms at first. Possible symptoms include feeling very thirsty, passing urine often, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing cuts and frequent infections. Anyone with these symptoms should book a check-up.

## Living with type 2 diabetes
Management usually combines healthy eating, regular physical activity, weight management, not smoking, and medicines when needed. Some people are asked to check their blood sugar at home; your care team will tell you if and when to test.

## Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)
Some diabetes medicines can make blood sugar drop too low. Signs include shaking, sweating, sudden hunger, a fast heartbeat, confusion and irritability. People at risk should follow the low-sugar plan agreed with their care team. Someone who becomes drowsy, confused or unconscious needs emergency help.

## Foot and eye care with diabetes
Diabetes can reduce feeling in the feet and damage the eyes. Check your feet every day for cuts, blisters or colour changes, wear comfortable shoes, and never walk barefoot outdoors. Have an eye check and a foot check at least once a year.

## Regular diabetes check-ups
Regular reviews usually include an HbA1c blood test, which shows average blood sugar over about three months, as well as blood pressure, cholesterol and kidney tests. Bring your medicine list and any home readings to each review.
