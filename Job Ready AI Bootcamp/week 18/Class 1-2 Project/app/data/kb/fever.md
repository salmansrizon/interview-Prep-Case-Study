# Fever

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## What counts as a fever
A fever is a body temperature of 38.0 °C (100.4 °F) or higher. Fever is the body's normal response to an infection and is not an illness by itself. How a person looks and behaves often tells you more than the exact number on the thermometer.

## Measuring a temperature
A digital thermometer is the most reliable choice at home. Use it in the mouth or under the arm for adults and older children, and under the arm for babies and young children. Follow the instructions that come with your thermometer. Touching the forehead is not an accurate way to measure a fever.

## Home care for a mild fever
Rest, drink plenty of fluids and wear light clothing. Do not wrap a person with a fever in heavy blankets. A fever-reducing medicine from a pharmacy can be used for comfort; follow the label for age and amount, and ask a pharmacist if you are unsure. The goal is comfort, not a normal number on the thermometer.

## Fever in babies and young children
Any fever in a baby younger than 3 months needs to be checked by a clinician straight away, even if the baby seems well. Older babies and children should be seen if they are unusually sleepy, are not drinking, have a rash that does not fade when pressed, or the fever lasts more than 3 days.

## When to seek care for a fever
Contact the clinic if a fever lasts more than 3 days, keeps coming back, or comes with a severe headache, stiff neck, rash, pain when passing urine, or confusion. Go to emergency care for a fever with difficulty breathing, a seizure, or a person who is very hard to wake.
