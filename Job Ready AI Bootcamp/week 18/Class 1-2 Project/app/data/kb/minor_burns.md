# Minor Burns

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## First aid for a minor burn
Cool the burn under cool or lukewarm running water for 20 minutes as soon as possible. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing near the burn before it swells, unless they are stuck to the skin. Keep the person warm while cooling the burn.

## What not to put on a burn
Do not use ice, iced water, butter, oil, toothpaste or other home remedies on a burn. They can damage the skin further or cause infection. Do not burst blisters.

## Covering a minor burn
After cooling, cover the burn loosely with cling film or a clean, non-fluffy dressing to protect it. A pharmacy pain reliever can be used for comfort; follow the label. Keep the area clean while it heals.

## When a burn needs medical care
See a clinician for burns larger than the person's palm, burns on the face, hands, feet, joints or genitals, deep burns that look white or charred, burns from chemicals or electricity, and any burn in a young child or older adult. Signs of infection, such as increasing redness, pus or fever, also need a check.
