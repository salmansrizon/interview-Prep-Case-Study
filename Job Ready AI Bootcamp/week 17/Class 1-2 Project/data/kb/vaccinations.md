# Vaccination Schedule Overview

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## Why vaccines matter
Vaccines train the immune system to protect against serious infections such as measles, polio, tetanus, diphtheria, whooping cough and some types of pneumonia. Vaccinating on time protects your child and helps protect babies and people who cannot be vaccinated.

## Childhood vaccines overview
Children in Bangladesh are vaccinated through the national Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI). In general, BCG and a first polio dose are given around birth, a series of combination vaccine doses starts at about 6 weeks of age with further doses a few weeks apart, and measles-rubella vaccine is given later in the first year with a second dose after that. Your child's vaccination card shows the exact schedule.

## Missed or late vaccine doses
If your child missed a vaccine, they can usually catch up; there is no need to start the whole series again. Bring the vaccination card to the clinic and a nurse will check which doses are due.

## Vaccines for adults
Adults may need a tetanus booster, a yearly flu vaccine (especially older adults, pregnant women and people with long-term conditions), and other vaccines depending on age, health, work or travel. Ask at your next appointment which vaccines are right for you.

## After a vaccination
Mild reactions are common and usually pass within one to two days: soreness or redness where the needle went in, a mild fever, or being irritable. Contact the clinic for a high fever or a reaction that lasts longer. Swelling of the face or difficulty breathing soon after a vaccine is an emergency.
