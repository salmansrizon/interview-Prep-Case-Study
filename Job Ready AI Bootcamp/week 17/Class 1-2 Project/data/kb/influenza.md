# Influenza (Flu)

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## Flu compared with a cold
Flu is caused by influenza viruses. It usually starts suddenly with fever, muscle aches, tiredness, headache and a dry cough. A cold usually starts slowly and is milder. Most healthy people recover from flu in about one to two weeks, but tiredness can last longer.

## Home care and stopping the spread of flu
Rest at home, drink plenty of fluids and stay away from others until you have had no fever for at least 24 hours without fever-reducing medicine. Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow and wash your hands often. Do not go to work or school while you have a fever.

## People at higher risk from flu
Flu can be more serious for adults over 65, pregnant women, young children, and people with long-term conditions such as asthma, diabetes, heart disease or a weak immune system. People in these groups should contact the clinic early when flu symptoms start, because a clinician may want to assess them. A yearly flu vaccine is the best protection.

## Flu warning signs
Seek care quickly for difficulty breathing, chest pain, confusion, severe weakness, a fever that comes back after improving, or signs of dehydration such as passing very little urine. In children, also watch for fast breathing, bluish lips and not drinking.
