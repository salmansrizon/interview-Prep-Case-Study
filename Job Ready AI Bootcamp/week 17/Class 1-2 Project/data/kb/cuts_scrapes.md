# Minor Cuts and Scrapes

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## Stopping minor bleeding
Wash your hands, then press firmly on the cut with a clean cloth or bandage for several minutes. Raise the injured part above the level of the heart if you can. Most small cuts stop bleeding within about 10 minutes of steady pressure.

## Cleaning a minor cut
Rinse the cut under clean running water and gently remove dirt. Cover it with a clean dressing or plaster and change it daily or when it gets wet or dirty. Keeping the wound clean and covered helps it heal.

## Signs a cut is infected
Watch for redness that spreads, swelling, warmth, increasing pain, pus or a fever. These can be signs of infection and need to be checked by a clinician.

## Tetanus protection after a cut
Tetanus is a serious infection that can enter through a wound, especially one made by a dirty or rusty object, or an animal bite. If you are not sure your tetanus vaccination is up to date, contact the clinic after the injury.

## When a cut needs stitches or medical care
A cut may need medical care if it is deep or gaping, longer than about 2 cm, on the face, caused by an animal or human bite, has something stuck in it, or keeps bleeding after 10 minutes of firm pressure.
