# Allergies

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## Seasonal allergies and hay fever
Allergic rhinitis, often called hay fever, causes sneezing, an itchy or runny nose and itchy, watery eyes. It is triggered by things like pollen, dust mites, mould or animal dander. Unlike a cold, it does not cause fever and can last for weeks while the trigger is around.

## Reducing allergy triggers
Keep windows closed on high-pollen days, wash bedding in hot water each week, reduce dust, shower and change clothes after being outdoors, and keep pets out of the bedroom if they trigger symptoms.

## Allergy medicines from the pharmacy
Antihistamine tablets, nasal sprays and eye drops are available from pharmacies. Some antihistamines cause drowsiness, so read the label before driving. Ask a pharmacist which product suits you, especially for children, during pregnancy or if you take other medicines.

## Severe allergic reactions
A severe allergic reaction, called anaphylaxis, can happen within minutes of a food, insect sting or medicine. Signs include swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, difficulty breathing, wheezing, feeling faint or collapsing. This is an emergency: call 999 straight away and use an adrenaline auto-injector if one has been prescribed.
