# Emergency Warning Signs

> Educational, fictional clinic handbook; not medical advice.

## When to call 999
Call 999, the national emergency number in Bangladesh, or go to the nearest emergency department straight away for any of the warning signs on this page. Do not wait for a clinic appointment and do not use telehealth or this assistant for an emergency.

## Chest pain and heart attack signs
Chest pain, pressure or tightness that lasts more than a few minutes, or spreads to the arm, neck, jaw or back, can be a heart attack. Other signs include shortness of breath, sweating, nausea and feeling faint. Call 999 straight away.

## Stroke signs: think FAST
FAST helps you spot a stroke. Face: is one side drooping? Arms: can the person lift both arms and keep them there? Speech: is it slurred or strange? Time: call 999 immediately. Every minute matters, even if the signs go away.

## Breathing difficulty
Severe difficulty breathing, gasping, being unable to speak in full sentences, or blue or grey lips are emergencies in adults and children. In children, also watch for the skin pulling in between the ribs with each breath.

## Severe bleeding
For heavy bleeding, press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth and keep pressing while someone calls 999. Bleeding that soaks through dressings, spurts, or does not slow down with firm pressure is an emergency, as is vomiting or coughing up blood.

## Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)
Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, difficulty breathing or swallowing, or collapse after a food, sting or medicine is an emergency. Call 999 and use an adrenaline auto-injector if the person has one.

## Unconsciousness and seizures
Call 999 if someone cannot be woken, has collapsed, or has a seizure that lasts more than five minutes, is their first seizure, or happens with an injury. Place an unconscious person who is breathing on their side.

## Fever in young babies
A temperature of 38.0 °C or higher in a baby younger than 3 months needs urgent medical assessment, even if the baby seems well. Also seek urgent care for any baby or child who is floppy, very hard to wake, or has a rash that does not fade when pressed.
